# Rendering the Reaches

A plan for generating a drawn map of the world from `content/`, in the spirit of the original
Midgaard map: roads drawn as roads with their names lettered along them, shops and halls drawn as
buildings fronting the road they open onto, and open country drawn as open country with a dashed
track through it.

Scope of the first cut is **Ossara** — 55 rooms, four zones. Everything here is written so the same
tool runs over the other four realms unchanged.

**Status: built, all five realms.** [tools/render-map.cs](../tools/render-map.cs) draws every sheet
in [content/map/](../content/map/). Phases 1–4 and 6 of §5 are done; phase 5 (the art sidecar) has
still not been needed — no room in the world required an override, so the maps are entirely derived.

```
dotnet run tools/render-map.cs -- --realm ossara -o content/map/ossara.svg
dotnet run tools/render-map.cs -- --realm ossara --model        # the derived model, no drawing
```

| Realm | Rooms | Sheet | Named runs | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ossara | 55 | 1105 × 5520 | 13 | Town, farm terraces, scrub, then the rim |
| Grask | 52 | 1335 × 5980 | 7 | A landing and market, then cutting and marsh |
| Azhen | 54 | 1105 × 5330 | 5 | Camp, then halls and ruined galleries |
| Nemhal | 56 | 1105 × 5495 | 1 | A hold, then farmland gone to ruin |
| The Unlit | 21 | 1105 × 3155 | 0 | Floors adrift in the dark |

Re-running on unchanged content is byte-identical for all five, which is checked rather than assumed.

## 1. What the content already gives us

Every claim in this section was checked against `content/ossara/*.json`, not assumed.

| Signal | Where | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Room title, description, key, zone | `rooms[]` | 55/55 |
| Exits with direction and target | `rooms[].exits` | 55/55, all six directions in use |
| A per-zone grid position | `rooms[].editorX/editorY` | 55/55, **no duplicate cells in any zone** |
| A 21×9 terrain grid + tile legend | `rooms[].grid`, `.legend` | 55/55 |
| `indoors` / `dark` / `peaceful` | `rooms[].flags`, `zones[].flags` | sparse, but exact where present |
| Who stands in the room | `spawners[].roomKeys` → `mobTemplates[]` | 8 NPCs in Gatetown |
| Whether that NPC is a shop, and what it sells | `mobTemplates[].behavior.shopkeeper`, `.sells` | 4 shops in Gatetown |

Two findings matter more than the rest, because they are what make an *artistic* render possible
without hand-authoring 224 rooms of art direction.

### 1.1 The zones stitch into one coordinate space, exactly

`editorX/editorY` are per-zone. But every zone boundary is crossed by a **seam exit** — a room in
one zone with an exit into another — and a seam pins the two zones' grids to each other. Solving
that by BFS over the zone graph gives Ossara a single global grid:

```
ossara.gatetown      offset (0,  0)
ossara.the-terraces  offset (0,  6)
ossara.brackenfell   offset (-1, 11)
ossara.the-rimwalk   offset (-1, 18)
```

**Zero offset conflicts and zero cell overlaps**, and the whole realm lands in a 4 × 24 bounding
box. The one dangling exit is `the-rim-gate --up--> grask.the-landing.the-gate-quay`, which is the
realm border and should be drawn as a border, not resolved.

This is the whole map projection, and it needs no new authoring. When a later realm *does* produce
a conflict — two seams disagreeing about where a zone sits — the tool must say so loudly and name
both seams rather than silently picking one. A conflict is a real fact about a zone that does not
lie flat, and the answer is either an authoring fix or an explicit override (§3.3), never a guess.

### 1.2 A room's terrain kind is recoverable, and it is the best classifier we have

Room grids were not drawn. They were generated by
[TerrainGenerator.cs](../src/Muwbta.Server/Building/TerrainGenerator.cs) from one of **21 kinds**,
seeded by the room key. The kind was never stored — but it is recoverable two ways:

- **By tile-set, both ways.** A kind is defined by its tiles, so the room's `legend` narrows it
  down. Subset alone is not enough — it lets `collapse` (floor, wall, rubble, rock) explain a room
  containing only `{floor, rock}`, and Brackenfell's heath comes out as three collapsed halls. An
  Enclosed kind always draws a wall border, so a room with no `wall` tile is not enclosed whatever
  else fits. **Requiring the kind's base and feature tiles to be present, as well as the room's
  tiles to be a subset of the kind's**, resolves **210 of 238 rooms uniquely**. The remaining 28
  are genuine ties that differ only in debris: `ruin`/`collapse` (21) and `thicket`/`scrub` (7).
- **By round-trip, where it works.** Generation is deterministic on the room key, so regenerating
  under each candidate and byte-comparing the grid should decide the ties. The tool links
  `Muwbta.Server` already (as `generate-terrain.cs` does), so this is a call, not a reimplementation.

  **It does not always work, and the tool says so.** `TerrainGenerator`'s own remarks admit the 21
  kinds are *reconstructed* from what `content/` happens to contain, and WORLD.md §10.1 names kinds
  (`field`, `pier`, `cave`) that the reconstruction does not have. A room drawn from a kind nobody
  reconstructed cannot round-trip. All seven `thicket`/`scrub` ties in Ossara fail it and are
  reported as warnings rather than silently resolved:

  ```
  warning: ossara.gatetown.the-common: {grass, tree} fits thicket/scrub and
           matches none of them byte-for-byte; taking thicket.
  ```

  This costs nothing today — the two candidates draw identically, both being grass with trees in
  it. It is recorded because the next realm's tie may not be so harmless.

Recovered kinds are startlingly on-the-nose for Gatetown:

| Room | Kind | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| North Lane, The Mustering Yard, The Terrace Gate | `street` | road |
| Market Row, The Provisioner's Stall | `market` | road with stalls |
| The Gate Road, The Cart Turn | `gateroad` | road through open ground |
| The Gate Yard | `hall` | plaza |
| The Armourer's Shop, Wick's Yard | `store` | building |
| Khaldra's Hearth, The Weaponwright's Shed | `taproom` | building |
| Ilvaro's House | `shrine` | building |
| The Low Wall | `rim` | the land stops |

**It is not sufficient on its own.** `The Root Cellar` is flagged `indoors` + `dark` and carries a
`{grass,tree}` tile-set; the terrain kind and the fiction disagree there. That is exactly the kind
of case §3.3 exists for.

## 2. What is missing

The data says where rooms are and what they are made of. It does not say:

1. **Which rooms are the same road.** "North Lane" and "Market Row" are separate rooms; on a map
   they are line segments that want a single lettered name each, and a road that ran three rooms
   long would want one name lettered along all three.
2. **Which road a building fronts.** Derivable — see §3.2 — but not stated.
3. **How big a thing is.** A shop and a quarry are one room each and should not be one size each.
4. **What fills the gaps.** Midgaard's charm is largely the unlabelled buildings packing the blocks
   between the named ones. We have to invent those, deterministically.
5. **What the realm is called on the sheet**, where the compass goes, where the border legends sit.

## 3. The data model: three layers

### 3.1 Layer 0 — the bundle, untouched

`content/**.json` is game data and stays game data. The renderer reads it and writes nothing back.
No `formatVersion` bump, no importer change, no risk to the live world.

Note that room `flags` is **not** an escape hatch for this: `RoomFlags` is a closed registry of
booleans ([RoomFlags.cs](../src/Muwbta.Domain/Worlds/RoomFlags.cs)), so `mapRole: "road"` cannot
live there. That is a good constraint and we should not fight it.

### 3.2 Layer 1 — derived, no authoring

Computed on every run from Layer 0:

- **Global placement** — the seam stitch of §1.1.
- **Terrain kind** — the round-trip of §1.2.
- **Degree** — out-exits and in-exits per room. Gatetown's five one-exit rooms are its five
  interiors; this is a sharp signal.
- **Occupancy** — shopkeeper present, what they sell, other NPCs, quest-giver.
- **Role**, by a priority cascade, first match wins:

  | # | Test | Role |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | a shopkeeper stands here, or the room is `indoors` | `building` |
  | 2 | kind ∈ {`hall`, `taproom`, `store`, `shrine`} | degree ≥ 3 ? `plaza` : `building` |
  | 3 | kind ∈ {`street`, `market`} | `road` |
  | 4 | kind ∈ {`gateroad`, `waste`} | `track` |
  | 5 | kind ∈ {`rim`, `brink`, `standing`} | `edge` |
  | 6 | kind ∈ {`ruin`, `collapse`} | `ruin` |
  | 7 | otherwise | `open` |
  | 8 | ...then in a **town** zone only, `open` with degree ≥ 3 is promoted | `plaza` |

  Two of these rules exist because the first draft got a room wrong, and both are worth keeping:

  - **Rule 2's demotion.** `The Gate Yard` is kind `hall` with four exits. Without it, the town's
    central square draws as a shop.
  - **Rule 1 beating rule 2.** `Wick's Yard` is kind `store` with three exits, so the demotion
    would make the general store a square. A shopkeeper is a premises whatever its degree, so the
    shop test runs first and does not consult degree at all.
  - **Rule 8.** `The Common` is grass with trees and four streets on it. Left as plain `open` it
    is a hole in the middle of Gatetown that cuts the main street in two. Open ground inside a
    town with three or more ways onto it is a square — drawn green rather than paved when its
    terrain kind is a grassy one, which is what makes a common look like a common.

- **Frontage** — a `building`'s road is the room its single exit leads to, and the exit's
  direction is which wall the door is in. A degree-1 room whose neighbour is a `road` is drawn
  attached to that road's edge. This is what puts "Weapon Shop" against Main Street on the
  Midgaard sheet.
- **Road runs** — chain `road`/`track` rooms that are collinear in the global grid into one
  polyline. The run carries the name of its **longest-named or highest-degree** member and the
  others become unlabelled continuations, which is how "Main Street" spans the whole sheet in the
  reference while the shops along it keep their own names.

  **Chaining requires an actual exit, never mere adjacency.** Ossara has nine pairs of rooms that
  sit in touching grid cells with no exit between them — The Armourer's Shop and The Weaponwright's
  Shed are side by side and not connected, because they are two separate buildings on the same
  block. That is correct and useful (it packs them as neighbours), but a run built from adjacency
  would weld them into a road.

  Three further rules, each earned by a bad label on the first render:

  - **A run never crosses a zone boundary.** Unbounded, Gatetown's main street runs on down through
    the Terraces and gets named "The High Shelf" after a farm shelf two zones away. A road name is
    a local thing.
  - **A square carries a run without naming it.** `The Gate Yard` sits in the middle of Gatetown's
    east–west street; excluded, that street becomes two one-room stubs.
  - **Prefer a name that is a way's name**, and where no unused member has one, leave a two-room
    run unlabelled. Otherwise the through-road takes its name for the longer run and the side
    street ends up lettered "THE MUSTERING YARD" after the dead-end it terminates in.

  In open country, runs go unlabelled entirely and each room gets its own italic name instead. A
  dashed track across a heath is a direction of travel and does not want a street name on it.

### 3.3 Layer 2 — the art sidecar, authored and optional

One file per realm: `content/map/ossara.json`. Not a bundle, not imported, keyed by room key, and
**every field optional** — an empty file must still produce a complete map.

```json
{
  "realm": "ossara",
  "title": "Ossara",
  "subtitle": "Ilvaro's country",
  "rooms": {
    "ossara.gatetown.the-gate-yard": { "role": "plaza", "label": "Gate Square", "emblem": "gate" },
    "ossara.gatetown.the-general-store": { "label": "Wick's — General Store", "footprint": [2, 1] },
    "ossara.the-terraces.the-root-cellar": { "vertical": "marker", "glyph": "cellar-stair" }
  },
  "zones": {
    "ossara.gatetown": { "pitch": 120, "density": "town" },
    "ossara.brackenfell": { "pitch": 260, "density": "wild" }
  },
  "borders": [
    { "from": "ossara.the-rimwalk.the-rim-gate", "direction": "up", "caption": "To Grask →" }
  ]
}
```

The rule that keeps this honest: **the sidecar overrides, it never supplies.** If deleting the
sidecar breaks the map, the derivation in §3.2 is wrong and should be fixed there. The sidecar is
for the dozen rooms where the fiction and the data genuinely disagree, plus the sheet furniture
(title, compass, border captions) that has no room to hang on.

## 4. Rendering

### 4.1 Output and tooling

`tools/render-map.cs`, a single-file `dotnet run` script like every other tool in that directory,
with `#:project ../src/Muwbta.Server/Muwbta.Server.csproj` so it can call `TerrainGenerator`
directly for §1.2.

```
dotnet run tools/render-map.cs -- --realm ossara -o build/maps/ossara.svg
```

**Output is SVG.** It is text, so it diffs, so a regenerated map is a readable change rather than
a binary blob. It scales to poster size, and PDF is a browser print away — which is how
`content/The Reaches Atlas.pdf` was already made (its metadata says Chrome). PNG via `resvg` or a
headless browser if we want raster.

**Determinism is a hard requirement**, exactly as it is for terrain: every random choice — filler
building placement, hatch jitter, tree scatter — is seeded from the room key. Re-running the tool
on unchanged content must produce a byte-identical file, or the map becomes un-reviewable.

### 4.2 The projection problem, and per-zone pitch

Ossara's global grid is 4 × 24 — a tall ribbon. Rendered at a uniform cell size that is a 1:6
sheet nobody wants to look at, and it is also *wrong*: Gatetown's 18 rooms are a few hundred paces
across and Brackenfell's 14 are a day's walk.

So **cell pitch is per zone**, from the `density` in §3.3 with a sensible default by zone level
band and room count:

- `town` — tight pitch (~120u), rooms drawn as building footprints and street segments packed edge
  to edge. Dense, like the top of the Midgaard sheet.
- `settled` — medium pitch, buildings present but with ground between them.
- `wild` — wide pitch (~260u), rooms drawn as regions rather than boxes, mostly ground.

Zone offsets from §1.1 are in *cells*, so converting to absolute units means accumulating pitch
along each axis rather than multiplying — the tool walks the stitched zone tree and lays out each
zone's block at its own scale, then packs the blocks. Where two zones of different pitch meet, the
seam is drawn as a road or track running between them, which is what a road does.

### 4.3 What each role draws

- **`road`** — a stroked band (two parallel lines, light fill) along the room's run polyline.
  Name set in the band on a `<textPath>` so it follows the road, letter-spaced, in the map's
  display face. Runs of one room get the name straight across.
- **`track`** — the same geometry but a **dashed centreline, no band**, which is the "open ground
  with a way through it" the reference asks for and is exactly what `gateroad`'s tile-set already
  says.
- **`building`** — a rectangle on the frontage road's edge, footprint from the sidecar or 1×1 by
  default, with a hatched or stippled fill. Name inside if it fits, otherwise in a leader-lined
  label outside. A shopkeeper's `sells` list picks a small glyph: blade, hammer, loaf, pack.
- **`plaza`** — an open paved polygon, name centred and letter-spaced wide.
- **`open`** — no outline. Ground texture only: the room's own terrain grid, sampled and scattered
  — tree tiles become tree marks, water becomes a hatched pond, rock becomes stipple. The room's
  name is set small and italic where there is space, or omitted, exactly as "The Park" and the
  unnamed paths are on the Midgaard sheet.
- **`edge`** — the outline of the land simply stops. Ground fill terminates in a ragged edge and
  there is nothing past it. No border, no cliff hatching, no label saying it is dangerous —
  §1.4 of WORLD.md (in the Reaches repository) is explicit that the horror is structural, and this is the one place
  the map can honour that directly.
- **`ruin`** — a broken rectangle, three walls and a gap, over stipple.

### 4.4 Filler

Blocks bounded by roads and not claimed by a named building get **unlabelled buildings**, packed
along the road frontage, seeded by the block's road-run keys so they are stable. Density follows
the zone's `density`. This is the single highest-value-per-line item in the whole plan: it is most
of what makes the reference image read as a city rather than a graph.

Filler must never overlap a real room's footprint, and must never appear in a `wild` zone.

### 4.5 The sheet

Border rule, realm title in a display face top-right, north arrow top-left, and border captions
from §3.3 at the dangling seams (`To Grask →`), all of which the reference does and all of which
are cheap.

### 4.6 Vertical exits, and what is on the other side

A diagonal nudge is what the builder canvas does with `up`/`down`
([layout.ts](../client/src/builder/layout.ts)) and it is the wrong answer for a map, because on a
map a diagonal is a claim about where a place *is*. **How a vertical exit draws depends on what it
opens into** — a cupboard, a cave system, or another Reach are three different pictures.

There are only ten vertical exits in the whole world, five bidirectional pairs, and they already
span the full range.

**The classifier is the size of the smaller side.** Cut the link, count what is still reachable
from each end, take the minimum. That number is how much of the world hangs off this one step:

| Smaller side | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A dead end. A cellar, a shaft, a shrine niche | **Point marker** (§4.6.1) |
| 2 – 8 | A small sub-level that continues | **Breakout inset** (§4.6.2) |
| > 8, one side | A whole region below | **Its own sheet**, cross-referenced |
| Large both sides | Not a level at all — a border | **Border crossing** (§4.6.3) |

Measured across the shipped world:

```
The Cellar Mouth  down -> The Root Cellar      smaller side = 1     point marker
The Rim Gate      up   -> The Gate Quay        55 / 183            border crossing
The First Workings up  -> The Gate Hall        107 / 131           border crossing
The Silent Room   up   -> The Hold Gate        77 / 161            border crossing
The Standing Place up  -> The Arrival          21 / 217            border crossing
```

**Nothing in the world today exercises the breakout case.** §4.6.2 is designed because caves and
sub-levels are obviously coming, not because anything needs it now — build it in phase 3 only if
content has arrived that wants it, and otherwise leave the hook and move on.

#### 4.6.1 Point markers

A vertical exit whose far side is a dead end never gets a cell of its own. It is drawn **on the
parent room** as a labelled glyph, the way the Midgaard sheet handles "Sewer Entrance" — a mark
inside The Dump rather than a room beside it.

The glyph comes from the **destination** room, in this order:

| Destination looks like | Glyph |
|---|---|
| `indoors` and `dark`, reached by `down` | stair descending into a dark opening |
| terrain kind `ruin` / `collapse`, or a natural-ground kind reached by `down` | cave arch |
| terrain kind `standing`, or the destination is in another realm | gate ring / portal |
| anything else reached by `up` | stair ascending |

Label is the destination's title, set beside the glyph — `The Root Cellar` reads on the sheet even
though the cellar itself is never drawn.

**This also fixes a bug the current projection would ship.** The Root Cellar is authored at
`editorX 0`, directly *west* of The Cellar Mouth at `editorX 1`, and linked to it by `up`. The
editor grid cannot distinguish "west of" from "below", so §1.1 taken literally would draw a cellar
as a building on the street. The renderer must **exclude vertically-linked rooms from the grid
projection entirely** and place them by their marker instead — and must not let their vacated cell
suppress filler (§4.4).

#### 4.6.2 Breakout insets

When the far side is small but continues, it gets drawn — just not in the parent's coordinate
space, because it is not in the parent's plane.

- The sub-level is laid out as its own miniature map, by the same §3.2 derivation and §4.3 role
  drawing, at the parent zone's pitch or tighter.
- It is placed in a **panel with its own rule and caption**, in whatever margin has room.
- Parent and panel are joined by a **matched symbol at both ends** — the same glyph from §4.6.1 on
  the parent room, repeated on the panel's entrance room — plus a leader arrow where the geometry
  allows one without crossing the map.

The matched symbol matters more than the arrow. An arrow across a sheet is noise; two identical
marks are read instantly and never collide with anything.

Within a sub-level, further `up`/`down` steps recurse through this same table.

#### 4.6.3 Border crossings

The four realm gates are not vertical movement and must not be drawn as it. They are the sheet's
edge.

Worth knowing before implementing: **all four use `up` on both sides.** Ossara's Rim Gate goes
`up` to Grask, and Grask's Gate Quay goes `up` back to Ossara. That is the fiction being literal —
the Reaches are shards and crossing is always up and out of one, never down into another — but it
means `up` is overloaded and no geometric reading of it is possible. **A cross-realm exit is
detected by the realm prefix of its target key and is never treated as vertical at all.**

They draw as the border captions of §4.5, with the portal glyph: a gate ring at the edge of the
land, `To Grask →`, and nothing past it.

## 5. Phases

Each phase ends in something you can look at.

1. **Extract.** `tools/render-map.cs` reading a realm, doing §1.1 stitch + §1.2 kind recovery +
   §3.2 derivation + the §4.6 vertical classification, and dumping the model as JSON. No drawing.
   Verify the roles by eye against the table in §1.2 — 18 rooms is a quick read.
2. **Skeleton SVG.** Every room a labelled box at global position, per-zone pitch, seams drawn,
   vertically-linked rooms held out of the grid. Ugly, correct, and it proves the projection.
3. **Roles.** Roads as bands with `textPath` names, buildings on their frontage, open ground as
   texture, edges that stop, point markers (§4.6.1) and border crossings (§4.6.3). This is the
   phase where it starts to look like the reference. Breakout panels (§4.6.2) only if content
   has arrived that needs one.
4. **Filler and furniture.** §4.4 and §4.5.
5. **Sidecar.** `content/map/ossara.json` and the override pass. Author only the rooms that
   phases 1–4 got wrong, and treat each one as a question about whether §3.2 could have got it
   right instead.
6. **The other four realms.** Run it, read the conflict report, fix what breaks. Grask's Stiltmarsh
   and the Unlit's `standing` rooms are where the projection assumptions will be tested.

   Done, and both predictions landed — see §5b. Grask's branching zones needed block separation and
   zone-scoped cell keys; the Unlit needed the sheet turned over. Azhen and Nemhal, unguessed,
   contributed the two drawing faults.

## 5a. What the first build taught

Kept here because each one is a rule the next realm will need, and none of them was predictable
from the data alone.

- **Labels need a collision map, and it has to know which way the text runs.** Names are drawn last
  in one pass, each claiming a box and stepping away from boxes already down. A run's name lettered
  along a *vertical* road claims a tall narrow box, not a wide flat one — claimed flat, "NORTH
  LANE" running down the road reserves nothing and the square's own name is set straight through it.
- **A `textPath` that overruns its path is silently clipped.** That is how "THE GATE ROAD" reaches
  the sheet as "HE GATE ROAD". Names are shrunk to fit, then skipped rather than clipped, and the
  start offset is clamped so the whole name stays on the road.
- **Estimating text width needs per-character advances.** A single average is fine for lowercase
  place names and much too narrow for a tracked, all-caps street name.
- **A run's name goes on the longest stretch of it that no square occupies**, not at its midpoint.
  In a town the midpoint of a road is very often the square, because that is where roads cross.
- **A skipped label must not silence the room.** When the renderer gives up on lettering a run, its
  rooms fall back to their own names — the model's intent to label a run is not evidence that a
  label was drawn.
- **Filler is ground, not decoration.** Unlabelled blocks have to join the land mass or they float
  on bare paper beside the town. They are found in the model and drawn in both passes.
- **"Which way does the land stop" needs the town's shape, not the first free side.** The Low Wall's
  first unoccupied neighbour is the block of houses east of it, so the naive answer tears Gatetown
  open sideways. Filler counts as ground, and of the free sides the one pointing furthest from the
  zone's centre wins.

## 5b. What the other four realms taught

Ossara was a gentle first case. Each of the others broke something the Ossara build had no reason
to get right.

### Grask — a realm need not lie flat

Ossara's four zones form a **chain**, so following the seams laid them out in a line and nothing
could collide. Grask **branches**: the Cutting has the Landing north of it, Stiltmarsh east and the
Owing south. A seam fixes a *direction and a contact point, not an extent* — so two branches of the
tree can want the same ground, and Stiltmarsh and the Owing come out drawn on top of each other.

Two separate things had to change:

- **A cell coordinate is only unique inside a zone.** The realm-wide `(x, y)` dictionaries the
  Ossara build used threw outright on Grask. Every cell lookup is now keyed by zone as well, which
  is also the honest semantics: two rooms in one cell of *one zone* is an authoring error, but the
  same cell of two different zones is just a drawing to be resolved.
- **Blocks are pushed apart until they stop overlapping**, along the axis of least overlap so the
  seam's own direction survives — a zone entered from the north stays south of its neighbour, just
  further south than the seam alone put it. The test is cell against cell, not bounding box against
  bounding box: a zone is rarely a rectangle, and two L-shaped zones can share a bounding box while
  no ground is actually contested. The tool warns when it has to do this, because a realm that does
  not lie flat is a fact worth knowing:

  ```
  warning: grask.the-owing: overlapped another zone and was pushed clear;
           this realm does not lie flat from its seams alone.
  ```

  Only Grask needs it. The other four resolve with no push at all.

- **A building is a building.** Grask's Tool Shed and Water Adit sit out in the Cutting, a `wild`
  zone drawn at a coarser pitch than the town. Scaled with the pitch they came out half again the
  size of the Landing's ironmonger, which says the shed is bigger, and it is not. Pitch decides
  where a building sits; it no longer decides how big it is.

### Azhen and Nemhal — a town with no streets in it

Neither realm contains a single `street` or `market` room, so neither has any `road` rooms at all.
They are halls and ruined galleries joined directly to one another, which is why Nemhal names
exactly one run across 56 rooms. This turned out to need no new rule: `Metalled` asks whether the
rooms are in a *town-density zone*, not whether they are roads, so hall connects to hall by a paved
way and the sheets read as building complexes rather than as failures.

Two drawing faults did surface, both from Ossara simply never having the case:

- **A road that runs to a building's centre caps in the open.** A round line cap reaches back past
  the point it is drawn to, so it surfaced on the far side of the roof as a blob. Ways now stop at
  the wall, retracted by the wall distance *plus* the cap radius.
- **Three standing walls read as an unfinished drawing.** Ossara has no ruins; Azhen has 20 and
  Nemhal 16. They now carry fallen rubble inside them, which is the whole difference between a ruin
  and a box somebody forgot to close.

### The Unlit — the one sheet that had to be turned over

All 21 rooms are `edge` (7 `rim`, 14 `standing`). Drawn by the normal rules, every room tore its own
outward side and the realm came out as a **chewed tan continent on cream paper** — which decorates
the idea instead of making it, exactly what §1.4 of WORLD.md (in the Reaches repository) and §10.1's note on
`standing` warn against.

So a realm more than a third `standing` is drawn **adrift**: the palette inverts, there is no land
mass to union, and each room is its own island of pale floor with dark all round it and loose grit
that came away and did not fall, because there is nowhere for it to fall to. The dashed tracks
between islands stop being paths across ground and become crossings over nothing.

This is the only conditional in the renderer that changes what the map *says* rather than how it
looks, and it earns that: the Unlit is the one place where the terrain was already making a point.

## 6. Decisions still open

- **Where breakout panels go.** §4.6.2 says "whatever margin has room", which is not an algorithm.
  Ossara's 4 × 24 ribbon has enormous margins and any greedy placement works; a squarer realm will
  not be so forgiving. Defer until content exists that needs a panel at all.
- ~~**Whether the map is a build artifact or a checked-in file.**~~ Decided: checked in, under
  `content/map/` (five SVGs, 67–194 KB each). Regeneration is byte-identical, so a change to a map
  is a readable diff rather than a new blob. A PNG of each sits beside it for people who just want
  to look at one.
- **Every sheet is a tall ribbon**, between 1:3 and 1:5. That is honest — each realm really is a
  corridor from its entry gate to its exit gate — but none of them is a shape you would print. A
  serpentine layout, folding the chain of zones back on itself, would square them up at the cost of
  the seam directions meaning what they say. Not attempted.
- **One sheet per realm, or one for the world.** Realm sheets first; a world index that shows the
  five realms and the gates between them is a different and much simpler drawing.
- **Whether the client eventually renders this.** Out of scope here. The SVG is a poster, not a
  minimap, and the two want different things.
